---
title: Figure
description: Figures with a muted caption, for images, diagrams and other self-contained content.
---

`figure` has no margin or padding. `figcaption` sits below the content in the muted color.

<Example title="Figure with image">
```html
<figure>
  <img
    alt="A gradient from blue to orange"
    width="960"
    height="300"
    src="data:image/svg+xml;utf8,%3Csvg xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg' width='960' height='300'%3E%3Cdefs%3E%3ClinearGradient id='g' x1='0' x2='1'%3E%3Cstop offset='0' stop-color='%234b6bfb'/%3E%3Cstop offset='1' stop-color='%23f2a65a'/%3E%3C/linearGradient%3E%3C/defs%3E%3Crect width='960' height='300' fill='url(%23g)'/%3E%3C/svg%3E"
  />
  <figcaption>A gradient standing in for a photo.</figcaption>
</figure>
```
</Example>

## Other content

A figure can hold an inline SVG, a table, a code block or a quote. The caption looks the same.

<Example title="Figure with SVG">
```html
<figure>
  <svg width="200" height="80" viewBox="0 0 200 80" role="img" aria-label="A circle in a frame">
    <rect x="4" y="4" width="192" height="72" rx="8" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" />
    <circle cx="40" cy="40" r="18" />
  </svg>
  <figcaption>An inline SVG that follows the text color.</figcaption>
</figure>
```
</Example>

## Accessibility

Give every `img` an `alt`. The caption does not replace it: use `alt` to describe the image and `figcaption` for context. Place `figcaption` as the first or last child of the `figure`.
