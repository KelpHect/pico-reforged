Form controls are styled by tag name and by `type`, so a plain form needs no classes. Inputs, selects and textareas are full width. A `label` can wrap its control or sit next to it, and `<small>` right after a control becomes helper text.

<Example title="A simple form">
```html
<form>
  <label>
    First name
    <input name="first_name" placeholder="First name" autocomplete="given-name" />
  </label>
  <label>
    Email
    <input type="email" name="email" placeholder="Email" autocomplete="email" />
    <small>We will never share your email.</small>
  </label>
  <label>
    Country
    <select name="country">
      <option>Portugal</option>
      <option>Spain</option>
    </select>
  </label>
  <fieldset>
    <label><input type="checkbox" name="terms" /> I agree to the terms</label>
    <label><input type="checkbox" role="switch" name="news" checked /> Send me news</label>
  </fieldset>
  <input type="submit" value="Subscribe" />
</form>
```
</Example>

## Labels, fieldsets and helper text

A `label` is a block with a small gap below it. Put the control inside the label or after it, and the spacing stays the same. Use `fieldset` and `legend` to group related controls. A fieldset has no border or padding, so it only adds structure and spacing.

`<small>` placed directly after an `input`, `select`, `textarea`, `fieldset` or `.grid` is pulled up under that element and takes the muted color. After a control with `aria-invalid`, it takes the valid or invalid color. See [Validation states](/docs/forms/validation).

## Disabled and read-only

`disabled` on a control, or on a `fieldset`, lowers the opacity and turns off pointer events. A `label` with `aria-disabled="true"` dims the same way. `readonly` keeps the normal look but skips the focus ring and the active background.

<Example title="Disabled and read-only" height={330}>
```html
<label>
  Disabled
  <input value="Cannot edit" disabled />
</label>
<label>
  Read-only
  <input value="Can select, cannot edit" readonly />
</label>
<fieldset disabled>
  <label>
    Inside a disabled fieldset
    <input value="Also disabled" />
  </label>
</fieldset>
```
</Example>
